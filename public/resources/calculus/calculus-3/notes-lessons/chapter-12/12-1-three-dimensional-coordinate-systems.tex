\section{Warm Up}

\textbf{Problem 1:} Find the distance between $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$ in two dimensions. Find the midpoint. 

\textbf{Problem 2:} What \emph{is} a circle? What is the equation of a circle centered at $(h,k)$ and radius $r$?

\section{Motivation}

We live in a 3-D world. When we say "3"-dimensions we mean that we can represent any point in our 3-D space with three numbers $(a,b,c)$. There are actually infinite ways we could set up a 3-D coordinate system (just like in 2-D) but if we were to create it ourselves, what would be the best way to do this? Can we see that a lot of what happens in 3-D is analogous to 2-D? 


\section{Definitions \& Theorems}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item  Rectangular coordinate system.
    \item  Right hand rule.
    \item Octants (Why are there 8?).
    \item  Projections. $(a,b,0), (a,0,c), (0,b,c)$.

\item  The \textbf{distance} between $(x_1,y_1,z_1)$ and $(x_1,y_2,z_2)$ is $\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2+(z_2-z_1)^2}$

\item  The \textbf{midpoint} of $(x_1,y_1,z_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2,z_2)$ is $(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2},\frac{z_1+z_2}{2})$.


\item The equations of a \textbf{sphere} with center $(h,k,l)$ and radius $r$ is $(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2+(z-l)^2 = r^2$.

\end{enumerate}
